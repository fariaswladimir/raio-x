# Barriers to AI Adoption in Micro Enterprises

## Introduction

Micro enterprises make up most of the businesses in the region studied, yet they adopt new technology more slowly than large firms. Cost is often cited as the main obstacle, but owners also mention a lack of time, unclear benefits and fear of losing control over customer data. This article examines these barriers in a survey of forty-one micro enterprises from retail, services and light manufacturing.

Respondents were asked whether they had tried AI tools, which tasks they would be willing to delegate and what would make them stop using a tool. The aim was not to measure the technology itself but to understand the decisions that surround it.

## Method

The survey combined closed questions with open comments. Answers were grouped by sector and by the number of employees. Open comments were read several times and coded according to the type of barrier mentioned. The researchers also reviewed the tasks that owners described as most time consuming, since these tasks are the most likely candidates for automation.

## Results

Nearly half of the owners had tried a general-purpose assistant at least once, but only a small share used one every week. The most common reason for stopping was that the tool created more tasks than it removed, for example by requiring constant corrections or additional checks. Owners who used agents reported better results when the agents were limited to one process, such as stock counts or payment reminders.

Concerns about data were less frequent than expected, yet they were stronger among owners who had already faced a security incident. Several respondents said they would only adopt a tool that stored information locally or within the country.

## Discussion

Barriers are connected. A tool that is cheap but demands many tasks of supervision can still be perceived as expensive. Support programs for micro enterprises should therefore focus on simple tasks, short training sessions and clear rules about data, rather than on the technology alone.
